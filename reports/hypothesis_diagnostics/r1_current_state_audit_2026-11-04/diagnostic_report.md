## What the experiment measures

The current R1 design tests multilingual-input question answering. The correct final answer is a canonical entity name used unchanged across languages; it does not test whether the model can phrase its answer fluently in French, German, Hindi, or Thai. The primary hypothesis concerns changes in two-hop final-answer accuracy after English-only unlearning of the first-hop fact. See [R1.tex](../../../docs/report/R1.tex).

## Literature scope check (2026-11-04)

The research question is plausible, but its novelty should be stated narrowly. Khandelwal et al.'s [CroLin-MQuAKE](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.701/) studies cross-lingual multi-hop knowledge **editing**, which is a close neighbor but changes/updates facts rather than removing an acquired intermediate fact. Recent [μ²-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20945) and [CLLPU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05976) study multilingual unlearning and language-boundary transfer using knowledge-unit QA and surface variants. Their published protocols do not appear to test whether unlearning one intermediate edge changes a dependent multi-hop final answer in another language. That distinction is an inference from the described protocols, not a systematic literature review.

These recent benchmarks also set a higher translation-quality bar: μ²-Bench describes human review of generated questions and human checks of back-translations; CLLPU describes blind back-translation, an independent model review, and a sampled human audit of accepted translations. R1's authorized AI-only translation review is materially weaker evidence and must remain labeled as such. It can support a feasibility diagnostic, but not a claim of human-validated multilingual ground truth.
